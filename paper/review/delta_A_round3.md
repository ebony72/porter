## New high-impact issues introduced or newly visible in the revision

### N01
- **Classification:** Major
- **Location:** Main paper, Algorithm 1, line 26; Section III-D, final paragraph under “Selection”
- **Triggering text:** Inside `while p* not ready`, the algorithm executes “abandon p*; continue,” while the prose says abandonment returns “to the top of the outer loop.”
- **Why this concerns a sceptical reviewer:** The nearest enclosing loop is the preparation loop. A conventional reading therefore repeats that loop without changing \(p^*\) or \(\ell\), recreating exactly the nontermination path the new branch was intended to eliminate. This is not merely stylistic because Algorithm 1 is presented as the executable control-flow specification.
- **Smallest credible fix:** Write “abandon \(p^*\); continue outer loop,” add a labelled outer loop, or set an abandonment flag, break the inner loop, and branch explicitly before execution.
- **Evidence needed:** No experiment. The pseudocode and prose must have identical control-flow semantics.

### N02
- **Classification:** Major
- **Location:** Main paper, Section IV-C, reproducibility paragraph; Section V-D, scale-up result; Section VIII, Conclusion
- **Triggering text:** Section IV-C says 360-qubit QPEexact can return either 1,224 or 2,342 EPR depending on whether the \(L_{tp}=128\) route fits a machine-dependent speculative wall-clock share, but Table V, the 507.7 geometric mean, the six-of-six claim, and the conclusion report only the 1,224 branch.
- **Why this concerns a sceptical reviewer:** The revision correctly discloses the instability but does not propagate it into the quantitative claims that depend on the unstable observation. Using the fallback value 2,342 changes the selected-series geometric mean from approximately 507.7 to approximately 564.0 and turns one of the six PORTER-versus-DSABRE wins into a loss. The qualitative horizon effect remains strong, but “wins all six” and the exact 0.72× aggregate are conditional rather than protocol-invariant.
- **Smallest credible fix:** Mark the affected Table V entry, report both possible aggregate outcomes and a five-of-six to six-of-six range, and qualify the conclusion as conditional on the long-horizon candidate completing. A stronger fix is to use a deterministic work budget or a sufficiently large per-candidate cap and regenerate the protocol results.
- **Evidence needed:** A reproducible selected-protocol run under a machine-independent stopping rule, or a sensitivity analysis computed from both documented outcomes.

### N03
- **Classification:** Moderate
- **Location:** Main paper, Section IV-C, wall-clock budget formula
- **Triggering text:** “Each speculative candidate gets \(\max(5,\lceil4t_{first}\rceil)\) seconds,” while the same paragraph reports a 14-second share and says the \(L_{tp}=128\) route needs less than one second more.
- **Why this concerns a sceptical reviewer:** If the stated formula is applied literally and \(t_{first}\) is measured in seconds, \(\lceil4t_{first}\rceil\) is an integer, so a 14-second share implies a different first-route-time range than a non-rounded share “miss[ing] by under a second.” The prose does not provide \(t_{first}\), the measured \(L=128\) time, or clarify whether the implementation uses the ceiling shown. The released probe may resolve this operationally, but the manuscript’s numerical account is not independently checkable as written.
- **Smallest credible fix:** Report the three measured values promised by the probe, namely \(t_{first}\), the resulting exact allocated share, and the \(L=128\) runtime, and ensure the displayed formula matches the implementation’s rounding.
- **Evidence needed:** The probe output for the released and confirming runs, or the corresponding exact values in the manuscript or appendix.
