## New issues introduced by the revision

### N01 · Major

**Location:** Section III-D, p. 5; Algorithm 1 line 26 on p. 3  
**Issue:** The new abandonment branch uses an unqualified `continue` inside the preparation `while` loop. In standard pseudocode this continues that inner loop, retaining the abandoned plan and unchanged layout, rather than returning to plan generation in the outer loop.  
**Reader impact:** A reader implementing the displayed algorithm obtains a repeated empty-preparation loop, directly contradicting the prose and the claimed preparation lifecycle.  
**Minimal revision direction:** Make the target control flow explicit. For example, set `abandoned <- true; break` at line 26, then after the inner loop execute `if abandoned then continue` in the outer loop; alternatively use a labelled `continue` whose target is the routing loop.

### N02 · Moderate

**Location:** Abstract, p. 1, penultimate sentence  
**Issue:** “Controlled ablations measure what each mechanism contributes” overstates both the experimental form and coverage. The revised paper itself distinguishes an ablation for relief, controlled layout and horizon comparisons, and mechanisms such as plan abstraction and commitment that are not isolated at all.  
**Reader impact:** The abstract leads readers to expect one-factor ablation evidence for every named design component, but Sections III-D, IV-A, V-C, V-D, and Appendix K explicitly provide a narrower evidence structure.  
**Minimal revision direction:** Replace with a scoped formulation such as: “Controlled experiments assess the contributions of capacity relief, action vocabulary, initial layout, and horizon selection, while plan abstraction and commitment remain unisolated.”
