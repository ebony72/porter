\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\PORTER{} is a deterministic distributed quantum circuit compiler whose
inter-core decision unit is a complete plan. It ranks the progress that a
remote action can deliver before generating plan-specific preparation SWAPs,
commits to the selected plan, and then prepares it. The implementation
combines this plan-oriented stage with capacity relief, a deterministic
initial layout, and a teleport-lookahead horizon $L_\mathrm{tp}$ chosen
per circuit from a fixed four-value portfolio.

The evaluation supports three qualified conclusions. First, under the
reported native protocols, \PORTER{} uses more than $40\%$ fewer EPR pairs
than \TeleSABRE{} on their common completed subsets and completes all 24
main-suite instances, compared with 17 for \TeleSABRE{}. Second, against
\dSABRE{}, \PORTER{} is competitive rather than uniformly superior: across
all 24 main-suite instances, it uses $0.99\times$ \dSABRE{}'s pooled EPR at
$1.20\times$ its complete compile time. \PORTER{}+R improves the pooled
EPR ratio to $0.91\times$, with a $2.15\times$ compile-time ratio. On the
reported QFT and QPEexact scale-up series, \PORTER{} uses $0.69\times$
\dSABRE{}'s EPR and $0.52\times$ its complete compile time.

Third, the controlled experiments identify important sensitivities. Capacity
relief is needed for completion on five of nine 80-qubit ablation instances.
Across the layout-by-router grid, the choice of initial layout affects
EPR more than the choice of routing loop on the reported non-trivial main
instances, although their route budgets remain different. On the scale-up series, routing at the configured horizon instead of
the four-value portfolio raises \dSABRE{}-relative EPR there to $1.45\times$;
because the portfolio was selected on the evaluation corpus, its benefit
requires out-of-sample validation.

The two compilers also obtain lower-EPR routes on different
instances: \PORTER{} records 11 wins, 10 losses, and 3 ties, while
\PORTER{}+R records 14 wins, 8 losses, and 2 ties. Taking the lower outcome
after running both compilers gives an oracle minimum of $150.5$ EPR pairs,
$12.5\%$ below \dSABRE{}'s pooled mean, but this is not a deployable
portfolio because it requires both runs and post-hoc knowledge of the
winner. Future work should establish completion and local-progress
guarantees, validate horizon selection on held-out workloads, calibrate the
plan comparator, and evaluate scheduling, fidelity, and interconnect
trade-offs under hardware-aware cost models
(Section~\ref{sec:future}).
