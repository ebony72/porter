\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\PORTER{} is a deterministic DQC compiler built on a plan-oriented remote
stage: it scores a complete inter-core action on the gate progress it
buys, before any preparation SWAP for it exists, and commits before
preparing. Around that it places a capacity discipline with two relief
tiers, a deterministic initial layout, and a \chg{teleport-lookahead horizon
chosen per circuit from a fixed four-value portfolio.}

Three conclusions are supported. First, on the evaluated MQT-Bench suites
\PORTER{} uses substantially fewer ($>40\%$) EPR pairs than published \TeleSABRE{} and completes all twenty-four
instances, including the seven on which \TeleSABRE{} does not converge. Second,
against \dSABRE{} it is competitive on those same suites,
$1.02\times$ its pooled EPR at \chg{$1.18\times$ its compile time under each}
compiler's own protocol, and its advantage grows with the machine,
reaching $0.72\times$ on the $100$--$360$-qubit scale-up series. Third,
controlled experiments locate where these results come from rather
than leaving them to the system as a whole, each by its own kind of
evidence: an ablation shows capacity relief is what lets a
deterministic-greedy design complete in practice; holding the initial
layout fixed shows it, not the routing loop, accounts for most of the
spread between the two compilers on those suites; and routing the
scale-up series at a fixed horizon instead of a selected one turns
$0.72\times$ into $1.45\times$, which is what carries \PORTER{} past
\dSABRE{} as the machine grows. The two compilers also
prove complementary rather than ranked: neither wins more than eleven of
the twenty-four instances, and taking the better of the two per instance
reaches \chg{$11.5\%$ below \dSABRE{} alone (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).}
