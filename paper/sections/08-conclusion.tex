\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\PORTER{} is a deterministic distributed quantum circuit compiler whose
inter-core decision unit is a complete plan. It ranks the progress that a
remote action can deliver before generating plan-specific preparation SWAPs,
commits to the selected plan, and then prepares it. The implementation
combines this plan-oriented stage with capacity relief, a deterministic
initial layout, and a teleport-lookahead horizon $L_\mathrm{tp}$ chosen
per circuit from a fixed four-value portfolio.

Under the reported native protocols, \PORTER{} uses substantially fewer EPR
pairs than \TeleSABRE{} and completes more instances, and it is competitive
with \dSABRE{} rather than uniformly superior: the two obtain their lower
counts on different instances, and backward refinement moves \PORTER{} ahead
on the pooled mean at a compile-time cost. The controlled experiments locate
the sensitivities. Capacity relief and gate teleportation are needed for
completion rather than for cost; the choice of initial layout affects EPR
more than the choice between the two routing loops; and the lookahead horizon
accounts for the scale-up outcome. Establishing completion and local-progress
guarantees, validating horizon selection out of sample, calibrating the plan
comparator, and evaluating scheduling, fidelity, and interconnect trade-offs
under hardware-aware cost models remain open
(Section~\ref{sec:future}).
