\section{Model, Primitives, and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Quantum Circuits and Routing}
\label{sec:qcircuits}

A circuit acts on $n$ logical qubits in the basis
$\{\texttt{id}, \texttt{rz}, \texttt{sx}, \texttt{x}, \texttt{cx}\}$ of
MQT-Bench's Qiskit-opt3-transpiled
circuits~\cite{quetschlich2023mqtbench} --- one-qubit gates plus CX ---
and is a directed acyclic graph with a dependency edge between
consecutive gates on each logical qubit. The \emph{front} is the set of
gates with no unexecuted predecessor; only front gates can execute or be
routed toward execution.

Physical qubits are partitioned into \emph{cores}. Each core has an
intra-core coupling graph, and pairs of cores may be joined by an
inter-core link whose endpoints are \emph{communication ports}. A
\emph{layout} maps every logical qubit to a physical position. A
two-qubit gate is \emph{local} if both operands share a core and
\emph{remote} otherwise: a local gate that is not yet adjacent needs
intra-core SWAPs, a remote gate needs an inter-core primitive. We write
$\kappa_\ell(q)$ for the core holding $q$ under layout $\ell$,
$\dL(c,p,p')$ for intra-core distance and $\dC(c,c')$ for core-graph
distance.

\subsection{Objective, Scope, and Assumptions}
\label{sec:scope}

\textbf{Objective.} Given a circuit DAG $C$ on $n$ logical qubits, a
machine $\arch$ of $K$ cores with intra-core coupling graphs, inter-core
links and designated communication ports, and an initial layout $\ell_0$,
produce a legal operation stream that realises every gate of $C$ while
minimising the number of EPR pairs consumed. Intra-core SWAP count,
circuit depth and compile time are recorded but are not part of the
objective and are never traded against EPR pairs by any rule in this
paper.

\textbf{Cost model.} One EPR pair is consumed per \TeleData{}, per
\TeleGate{} and per \TeleGatePacket{} --- one per packet, however many
remote CX gates it amortises --- charged the same whichever link carries
it; a multi-hop relocation is a sequence of single-hop \TeleData{}
actions, charged once per hop. This is the model
\TeleSABRE{}~\cite{russo2025telesabre} and \dSABRE{}~\cite{lidsabre} use,
which is what makes the three compilers comparable in one unit.
Communication ports are ordinary physical qubits, occupied for an
action's duration and free otherwise; no core is assumed to have
dedicated communication memory. What the model leaves out --- link
contention, entanglement latency and fidelity, purification, classical
communication --- is outside it, and every conclusion here is relative to
that model. \appThreats{} collects this and the evaluation's other
assumptions in one place.

\textbf{State teleportation (\TeleData{}).} Consumes one EPR pair to
move a logical qubit from a communication port in one core to a free
port in another, after which every later gate that qubit meets in its
new core is local. Its value is therefore \emph{latent}: it is paid
once and repaid over however many future gates the relocation makes
local.

\textbf{Gate teleportation (\TeleGate{}).} Executes a single remote CX
with neither operand moving. One EPR pair entangles the two communication
ports; a \emph{cat-entangler} leaves $(q_c,e_B)$ in the cat state
$\alpha|00\rangle + \beta|11\rangle$, so every CX controlled by the
remote port $e_B$ acts as if controlled by $q_c$, and a
\emph{cat-disentangler} then restores $q_c$ and frees both ports
(\appCatent{} gives both constructions). Its value is entirely
\emph{direct}: exactly one gate, nothing left behind.

\textbf{Same-control packets (\TeleGatePacket{}).} When several remote
CX gates share the same control and their targets lie in one core, the
run can be bracketed between a \emph{single} cat-entangler and
disentangler: the ports stay reserved, each target is routed beside
$e_B$ in turn, and the whole run costs one EPR pair instead of one
each. This is the amortisation idea of AutoComm~\cite{wu2022autocomm},
specialised to the shared-control case and applied inside a routing
loop rather than as a pre-pass.

The three differ in the respect that matters for scoring: how many gates
one pair is spread over. \TeleData{} amortises over an unknown future,
\TeleGate{} over exactly one gate, \TeleGatePacket{} over a run whose
length the compiler must discover. Section~\ref{sec:sfc-scoring} makes
that a direct/latent/lookahead decomposition.
