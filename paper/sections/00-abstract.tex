\begin{abstract}
A distributed quantum computer runs one circuit across several small cores
joined by links that distribute entanglement on demand; under the usual
cost model the compiler minimises the Einstein--Podolsky--Rosen (EPR)
pairs spent on gates that straddle cores. Some existing routers fold into an inter-core action's score
the preparation the current layout demands for it, so its apparent value
depends on the layout the compiler happens to occupy. We present \PORTER{}, which instead
ranks complete inter-core \emph{plans} --- which primitive fires, which
gates it resolves, which readiness conditions it needs --- on the gate
progress each buys, letting preparation cost only break ties, then commits
to one and prepares it. It supports \chg{the three inter-core primitives studied under this
cost model}, including cat-entangler packets that amortise one pair over a
run of remote gates sharing a control, and adds capacity-aware relief, a
deterministic initial layout, and a per-circuit choice of
teleport-lookahead horizon. On MQT-Bench suites at 25, 64 and 80 logical
qubits \PORTER{} uses $59.6\%$, $41.6\%$ and $68.2\%$ fewer EPR pairs in
geometric mean than published \TeleSABRE{} at three routes each\chg{, over
the instances both complete}, and completes all twenty-four instances to
\TeleSABRE{}'s seventeen. Against \dSABRE{} it is competitive on those suites
--- $1.02\times$ its pooled EPR at $0.91\times$ its compile time, each
under its own protocol --- and pulls ahead as the machine grows, using
$0.72\times$ its pairs on a $100$--$360$-qubit \chg{QFT and QPEexact}
scale-up series.
The two prove complementary rather than ranked, and taking the better of
the two per instance uses $9.3\%$ fewer pairs than \dSABRE{} alone.
\chg{Controlled experiments separate the contributions of capacity
relief, the set of primitives, the initial layout and the horizon; relief
earns its place on completion rather than cost, five of nine 80-qubit
circuits failing outright without it.}
The implementation, harness and raw results are public.
\end{abstract}
