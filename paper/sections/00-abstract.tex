\begin{abstract}
A distributed quantum computer runs one circuit across several small
cores joined by links that distribute entanglement on demand, and under
the usual cost model the compiler's objective is to spend as few
Einstein--Podolsky--Rosen (EPR) pairs as possible on gates that straddle
cores. Existing routers decide among inter-core actions while
simultaneously staging them, so an action's apparent value depends on the
layout the compiler happens to occupy. We present \PORTER{}, a compiler
that instead scores a complete inter-core \emph{plan} --- which primitive
fires, which gates it resolves, and which readiness conditions it needs
--- before any preparation SWAP for it exists, commits to one, and only
then generates that plan's preparation. Around this it adds a two-tier
capacity-relief mechanism, a deterministic seed-free initial layout, and a
per-circuit choice of teleport-lookahead horizon. \PORTER{} unifies the
three known inter-core primitives, including cat-entangler packets that
amortise one pair across a run of remote gates sharing a control.
On MQT-Bench suites at 25, 64 and 80 logical qubits \PORTER{} uses
$59.6\%$, $41.6\%$ and $68.2\%$ fewer EPR pairs in geometric mean than
published \TeleSABRE{} at a matched three-attempt budget, and completes
all twenty-four instances where \TeleSABRE{} completes seventeen. Against
\dSABRE{}, concurrent work whose router was revised during this study and
which we therefore re-ran from a pinned revision, \PORTER{} is
competitive, and which compiler leads depends on the operating point:
under each compiler's own protocol \PORTER{} uses $7\%$ more pooled EPR
at $0.58\times$ the compile time, a comparable search budget reverses
that, and \PORTER{} leads on identical externally supplied layouts, on
both heavy-hex core graphs and on a $100$--$360$-qubit scale-up series.
Controlled ablations attribute the results: capacity relief is what makes
a deterministic-greedy design complete in practice, the initial layout
dominates below roughly a hundred physical qubits and the lookahead
horizon above it, and plan commitment reaches the same EPR as re-scoring
every iteration while costing less time. A richer gate-teleportation
vocabulary does not always help, which we report as measured. Every
\PORTER{} route is certified by a separately implemented replay verifier;
the implementation, verifier, harness and raw results are public.
\end{abstract}
