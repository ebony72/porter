\begin{abstract}
A distributed quantum computer runs one circuit across several small cores
joined by links that distribute entanglement on demand. Under the usual
cost model the compiler's objective is to spend as few
Einstein--Podolsky--Rosen (EPR) pairs as possible on gates that straddle
cores. Existing routers choose among inter-core actions while
simultaneously staging them, so an action's apparent value depends on the
layout the compiler happens to occupy. We present \PORTER{}, a compiler
that instead scores a complete inter-core \emph{plan} --- which primitive
fires, which gates it resolves, and which readiness conditions it needs
--- before any preparation SWAP for it exists, commits to one, and only
then prepares it. \PORTER{} supports all three known inter-core
primitives, including cat-entangler packets that amortise one pair over a
run of remote gates sharing a control, and adds capacity-aware relief, a
deterministic seed-free initial layout, and a per-circuit choice of
teleport-lookahead horizon. On MQT-Bench suites at 25, 64 and 80 logical
qubits \PORTER{} uses $59.6\%$, $41.6\%$ and $68.2\%$ fewer EPR pairs in
geometric mean than published \TeleSABRE{} at a matched attempt budget,
and completes all twenty-four instances where \TeleSABRE{} completes
seventeen. Against \dSABRE{}, \PORTER{} is competitive, and which
compiler leads depends on the operating point: it uses $7\%$ more pooled
EPR at $0.58\times$ the compile time under each compiler's own protocol,
and leads once the initial layout is controlled and at the largest scales
evaluated.
Controlled ablations attribute these results to individual mechanisms and
report two negative findings that bound them. Every \PORTER{} route is
certified by a separately implemented replay verifier; the
implementation, verifier, harness and raw results are public.
\end{abstract}
