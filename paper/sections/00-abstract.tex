\begin{abstract}
A distributed quantum computer runs one circuit across several small cores
joined by links that distribute entanglement on demand. Under the usual
cost model the compiler's objective is to spend as few
Einstein--Podolsky--Rosen (EPR) pairs as possible on gates that straddle
cores. Existing routers choose among inter-core actions while
simultaneously staging them, so an action's apparent value depends on the
layout the compiler happens to occupy. We present \PORTER{}, a compiler
that instead scores a complete inter-core \emph{plan} --- which primitive
fires, which gates it resolves, and which readiness conditions it needs
--- before any preparation SWAP for it exists, commits to one, and only
then prepares it. \PORTER{} supports \chg{the three inter-core primitives
studied under this cost model}, including cat-entangler packets that amortise one pair over a
run of remote gates sharing a control, and adds capacity-aware relief, a
deterministic initial layout, and a per-circuit choice of
teleport-lookahead horizon. On MQT-Bench suites at 25, 64 and 80 logical
qubits \PORTER{} uses $59.6\%$, $41.6\%$ and $68.2\%$ fewer EPR pairs in
geometric mean than published \TeleSABRE{} at three routes each\chg{, over
the instances both complete}, and completes all twenty-four instances where
\TeleSABRE{} completes seventeen. Against \dSABRE{} it is competitive at the sizes both target
--- $1.02\times$ its pooled EPR at $0.91\times$ its compile time under
each compiler's own protocol --- and pulls ahead as the machine grows,
using $0.72\times$ its EPR pairs on a $100$--$360$-qubit scale-up series
\chg{of QFT and QPEexact}.
The two prove complementary rather than ranked: neither wins more than
eleven of the twenty-four instances, and taking the better of the two per
instance \chg{--- an oracle over both, costing both --- }uses $9.3\%$ fewer
pairs than \dSABRE{} alone, so the designs reach different circuits rather
than re-deriving each other.
Controlled ablations measure what each mechanism contributes.
The implementation, harness and raw results are public.
\end{abstract}
