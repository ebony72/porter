The Horizon's Second Job

dag_extended_max_layers is documented as how far ahead the router looks. It is also, undocumented, how heavily the future outweighs the present — because the lookahead term is a sum and the direct term is not. That second job is what makes deep horizons unpredictable, and taken to its limit it is every timeout PORTER has.

The mechanism, in one decision

The lookahead is exactly antisymmetric

From a frozen route — main/64/qft at L=128 — two plans for the same qubit, scored in the same iteration:

Reversing a move negates the lookahead term exactly: the walk collects the same gates with the same weights, and a local SWAP never changes a qubit's core. The direct term — the only part tied to actually retiring a gate — is outweighed 11:1.

Antisymmetry alone would be harmless: the reverse of a good move scores badly. What closes the loop is that the remote stage has no null action. Every iteration it must commit to some plan. At core 2 all nine candidates scored negative, and the least-bad of them was the undo of the move just made:

Candidate at core 2directlookaheadtotal

From core 5 the return scores +12.16 outright. So the router shuttles: at one end the move is the best available, at the other its undo is the least bad. The traced tail is exactly periodic with period 3 — one local SWAP, one teleport, nothing retired — repeated 38,741 times.

The failure rate is monotone in the horizon, and across the released weight-schedule arms it tracks the lookahead's magnitude rather than the circuit mix — the arms whose sum saturates instead of accumulating fail eight times less often.

The compiler does not fail; candidate routes do. The protocol keeps the best of four horizons, so PORTER completes 48/48 while 8.3% of individual routes do not. But the margin is thinner than it looks: 35 instances complete on all four candidates, 12 on three, and 80q Random on only two.

Question 1 · when to go deeper

No rule survives contact with the data

Deep pays on QFT/QPEexact at scale and hurts on QAOA/QNN — but the split is not a circuit property. The same 64-qubit QFT wants L=16 on architecture H and L=256 on both heavy-hex graphs.

refuted

Partner novelty. Distinct partners per collected gate is ~0.51 for QAOA, QFT, QNN and the scale-up series. The deep-helps and deep-harms groups are indistinguishable.

refuted

Circuit depth, the best static signal. Spearman +0.68 against the deep-vs-shallow ratio (density −0.60, cores +0.04, diameter +0.04) — but the best L*/D spans 420×.

refuted

Any single-threshold gate. Exhaustive search over six features, both directions, every threshold: in-sample best is 373.3, which is exactly what ungated {16,128} scores. The best gate the data admits never usefully fires.

refuted

A runtime test. The domination ratio measured free on the first route correlates +0.02 with whether deeper pays. A seven-instance eyeball looked promising; on all 38 it is noise.

and yet

There is almost nothing to win. The shipped four-route portfolio scores 361.6; an oracle over all eight horizons scores 350.4. A perfect predictor is worth 3.1%.

Normalising is a completion fix that costs EPR, not an EPR win: it fixes 28 cells and breaks 2, but is 17 better / 20 worse across the portfolio, and it does not flatten the horizon enough to shrink the candidate set.

The narrower flag: forbid the undo

teleport_revisit_block drops a teleport that would return a qubit to a core it has already occupied since the last gate retirement. The record clears on every retirement, so it constrains only the no-progress episode it is trying to end — and between two retirements each qubit may enter each core at most once, bounding the remote stage's wasted work.

Recommendation

What I would and would not change

keep

The four-horizon portfolio, unchanged. It is within 3.1% of an eight-horizon oracle, and adding the other three horizons buys 1.3% for three more routes. Nothing in this investigation justifies touching it.

stop

Searching for a depth predictor. Four independent families are now refuted. The 3.1% ceiling makes the whole exercise worth less than the routes it would cost to evaluate.

record

That the horizon carries a hidden weight. This is the missing half of §VI-A's open item — "a scale on which a resolved gate and a latent distance reduction are commensurable … would have to come from the summation". It does, and sqrt is that scale.

refine

The completion argument. §VI says a guarantee "needs a progress argument for the intra-core stage". True of the configurations the paper reports — but across the horizon grid the dominant failure is a remote-stage shuttle, 28 cells to 2. The remote stage has its own livelock, and unlike the intra-core one it now has an identified cause and a bound.
